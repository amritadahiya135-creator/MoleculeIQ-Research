# Known Limitations and Boundaries

**Product:** MoleculeIQ  
**Classification:** Research, Academic and Educational Decision-Support Prototype  
**Version:** Stage 2 Hardened Implementation  

---

## 1. Regulatory and Clinical Disclaimers
1. MoleculeIQ is **not** a clinical diagnostic system or a medical device.
2. Predictions generated by the system do **not** constitute regulatory toxicology filings, safety certificates, or efficacy guarantees.
3. Computational outputs must **never** replace empirical wet-lab testing (e.g. AMES assays, patch-clamp hERG, animal models, or clinical trials).

---

## 2. In Silico Modeling Boundaries
1. **Benchmark Context & Label Semantics:** 
   - ClinTox models predict the likelihood of clinical development failure attributed to toxicity based on historical MoleculeNet records.
   - *Historical Nuance (e.g., Aspirin):* Established therapeutics such as Aspirin are labeled `FDA_APPROVED=0, CT_TOX=1` in raw ClinTox due to trial discontinuation events in non-standard or alternative clinical trial settings. High predicted toxicity for such compounds reflects historical trial records, not everyday consumer safety.
   - *ClinTox Multi-Seed Generalization & Threshold Sensitivity:* In multi-seed 5-fold cross-validation across 3 seeds (30 fits), scaffold-grouped ROC-AUC ($0.8473$) and random-split ROC-AUC ($0.8410$) show negligible difference ($\Delta = +0.0063$, $\Delta \text{PR-AUC} = -0.0014$), diluted by ClinTox's high scaffold sparsity ($81.1\%$ singleton scaffolds among 873 total groups, with 129 acyclic compounds across 90 generic skeleton groups). At default $\tau = 0.50$, scaffold recall is only $19.33\%$. While an exploratory training-prevalence threshold ($\tau \approx 0.077$) achieves $95.53\%$ recall, specificity drops to $32.76\%$ with precision at $10.60\%$. Neither threshold is validated for real-world toxicology decisions.
   - Tox21 models predict in vitro pathway activation in specific cell-based reporter assays across 12 targets. Positive assay responses do not necessarily translate into in vivo organ toxicity or human adverse drug events.
2. **Chemical Space & Dynamic Applicability Domain:**
   - The ClinTox model is trained on small drug-like organic molecules ($MW < 600$ Da, median nearest-neighbor Tanimoto $= 0.55$). Molecules with maximum ClinTox training set Tanimoto similarity $< 0.25$ (such as platinum chemotherapeutics like Cisplatin, inorganic fluorides like $\text{XeF}_2$, or organometallics) fall **outside the applicability domain** and receive an explicit warning indicating extrapolation.
   - For Tox21, applicability domain scores reflect maximum Tanimoto similarity strictly against each endpoint's isolated training partition. Because empirical validation across diverse in-vitro cellular targets is lacking, Tox21 does not apply arbitrary categorical boundaries; status remains `UNVALIDATED`. High similarity indicates structural coverage in training data, but does **not** guarantee biological inactivity or safety.
3. **2D Topological Graph vs. 3D Conformation:**
   - 2D Morgan circular fingerprints ignore 3D stereochemical conformations, solvent interactions, membrane permeation dynamics, and protein target pocket fit.
4. **Metabolism Invisibility:**
   - The platform evaluates parent chemical structures directly. Toxicities resulting from cytochrome P450 bioactivation (e.g., acetaminophen hepatotoxicity via NAPQI) are not simulated.

## 3. Probability Calibration Boundaries
1. **ClinTox Platt Scaling:** While Platt scaling improves probability alignment with historical prevalence (cutting Brier score to $0.0522$), predicted probabilities represent population-level risk of trial failure, not individualized patient toxicology or organ-specific necrosis likelihood.
2. **Tox21 12-Endpoint Post-Hoc Calibration (Phase B.1 — Schema v1.1.0):**
   - **3-Way Disjoint Selection & 5-Fold OOF Fitting:** Calibration method selection uses a 5-fold 3-way disjoint training split (`base_rf_idx` [60%] $\perp$ `cal_fit_idx` [20%] $\perp$ `val_idx` [20%]) with conservative Sigmoid fallback when per-fold calibration-fitting positive support is $< 200$, selecting Sigmoid (`sigmoid`) across all 12 endpoints. Final parameters are fitted on 5-fold stratified OOF training predictions. On the locked 20% test partitions ($N_{\text{test, total}} = 15,577$), Sigmoid reduces macro-average Brier score from $0.0598$ to $0.0523$ ($-12.5\%$), Log Loss from $0.2461$ to $0.1916$ ($-22.1\%$), and 10-bin weighted Expected Calibration Error ($\text{ECE}$) from $0.0618$ to $0.0134$ ($-78.3\%$) while strictly preserving ROC-AUC ($0.8337$) and PR-AUC ($0.4444$).
   - **Point-Estimate vs. Statistically Supported Improvement on Rare-Positive Assays:** While Sigmoid reduces point-estimate test Brier score, Log Loss, and ECE across all 12 endpoints, paired 95% bootstrap confidence intervals ($B = 500$) for $\Delta \text{Brier}$ exclude zero on **10 of 12 endpoints** and include zero on the two rarest endpoints: `NR-PPAR-gamma` ($N_{\text{test, pos}} = 37$, $\Delta \text{Brier} = -0.0014$, 95% CI $[-0.0033, +0.0009]$) and `SR-ATAD5` ($N_{\text{test, pos}} = 53$, $\Delta \text{Brier} = -0.0007$, 95% CI $[-0.0030, +0.0017]$).
   - **Distinction Between Calibration and Classification Thresholds:** Because Tox21 assays have low empirical positive base rates ($2.89\%\text{–}16.17\%$), calibrated probabilities compress toward the true prevalence. Applying an unadjusted $0.50$ threshold to calibrated probabilities reduces binary sensitivity on rare-positive assays. Therefore, MoleculeIQ retains raw `class_weight='balanced'` Random Forest probabilities (`probability`) as the default for binary activity classification (`is_active` at $\ge 0.50$) and composite candidate ranking, while exposing calibrated probabilities (`calibrated_probability`) as opt-in empirical likelihood estimates.
   - **Biological, Prospective & Out-of-Domain Boundaries:** Post-hoc probability calibration corrects for training-prevalence shift within the i.i.d. benchmark distribution; it does **not** establish clinical safety, causal mechanism, prospective wet-lab assay performance, or reliable probability estimates outside the model's chemical applicability domain (low Tanimoto similarity to the endpoint training corpus).

## 4. Explainability Boundaries
1. SHAP values represent statistical feature attributions within the Random Forest model. A high positive SHAP value for a fingerprint bit indicates that the bit increased the model's toxicity score; it is **not** biochemical proof of a toxicophore mechanism or causal toxicity pathway.

---

## 5. Literature Search Boundaries
1. PubMed retrieval queries NCBI public endpoints and extracts abstract text. Full-text articles behind paywalls cannot be indexed.
2. Cosine similarity of sentence embeddings highlights semantic relevance to the query, but does not verify whether the cited experimental findings are reproducible, peer-reviewed, or consensus truth.
