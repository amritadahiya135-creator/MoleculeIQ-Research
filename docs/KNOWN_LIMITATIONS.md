# Known Limitations and Boundaries

**Product:** MoleculeIQ  
**Classification:** Research, Academic and Educational Decision-Support Prototype  

---

## 1. Regulatory and Clinical Disclaimers
1. MoleculeIQ is **not** a clinical diagnostic system or a medical device.
2. Predictions generated by the system do **not** constitute regulatory toxicology filings, safety certificates, or efficacy guarantees.
3. Computational outputs must **never** replace empirical wet-lab testing (e.g. AMES assays, patch-clamp hERG, animal models, or clinical trials).

## 2. In Silico Modeling Boundaries
1. **Benchmark Context:** 
   - ClinTox models predict the likelihood of clinical development failure attributed to toxicity based on historical MoleculeNet records. Many molecules that pass clinical trials still display adverse effects in specific patient cohorts.
   - Tox21 models predict in vitro pathway activation in specific cell-based reporter assays. Positive assay responses do not necessarily translate into in vivo organ toxicity or human mortality.
2. **Chemical Space Generalization:**
   - Models trained on historical small molecules may suffer accuracy degradation when evaluated on structurally novel chemical matter, macrocycles, natural products, or inorganic complexes outside their applicability domain.
3. **2D vs. 3D Conformation:**
   - 2D Morgan circular fingerprints ignore 3D stereochemical conformations, solvent interactions, membrane permeation dynamics, and protein target pocket fit.

## 3. Explainability Boundaries
1. SHAP values represent statistical feature attributions within the Random Forest model. A high positive SHAP value for a fingerprint bit indicates that the bit increased the model's toxicity score; it is **not** biochemical proof of a toxicophore mechanism or causal toxicity pathway.

## 4. Literature Search Boundaries
1. PubMed retrieval queries NCBI public endpoints and extracts abstract text. Full-text articles behind paywalls cannot be indexed.
2. Cosine similarity of sentence embeddings highlights semantic relevance to the query, but does not verify whether the cited experimental findings are reproducible, peer-reviewed, or consensus truth.
